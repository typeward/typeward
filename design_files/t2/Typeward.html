§ 2   On Privacy

The quiet case for keeping your work close.

Abstract We consider the writing environment as a closed system. The author's text, figures, and revisions need never leave the machine on which they are composed. Where cloud editors introduce latency, surveillance, and the quiet anxiety of a server that might disappear, a local-first tool returns the document to its rightful owner. We argue, with proof, that calm and control are not opposed to capability.
Def. 2.1
Definition 2.1 (Local-first).
An editor is local-first if every document it produces is readable, editable, and complete on the author's own disk, without a network connection and without permission from any third party.
Thm. 4.2
Theorem 4.2 (Calm preserved).
If a tool stores plain .tex files locally and compiles on the author's hardware, then it introduces no external dependency, no queue, and no outage into the act of writing.
Proof.
By construction. The source is a folder of text. The compiler is a single binary. Each is owned by the author and answers to nothing else. The conditions of Definition 2.1 are met directly, and there is nothing left to depend on.
§ 3   Performance

Compiles before you finish your thought.

No build queue. No "your document is 14th in line." Typeward runs the Tectonic engine directly on your hardware, so the page renders the instant you stop typing.

Figure 1: Compile time vs. document length. Cloud build times grow until they hit the timeout; local compilation stays flat.
  • 3.1

    No queue, ever

    Your document compiles the moment you save — it is never waiting behind anyone else's.

  • 3.2

    Incremental builds

    Only what changed is recompiled. A one-line edit to a 200-page thesis renders in milliseconds.

  • 3.3

    Offline by default

    On a plane, in a library, off the grid — the compiler is already on your disk. Nothing to reach for.
